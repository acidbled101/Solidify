Post-processing · the repair ladder

Try the best method first.
Fall only as far as you must.

Every rung down buys a watertight mesh with quality. The pipeline starts at the top and stops the moment a slicer would accept the result — and tells the user, live, which rung it is on and why the one above failed.

1

Manifold3D accepts it as-is

Exact predicate test on an oriented 2-manifold. If every edge is shared by exactly two faces and winding is coherent, no work is needed.
nothing — the mesh is already valid
2

PyMeshLab repairs non-manifold edges

Topological split of non-manifold edges and vertices, then hole-filling on boundary loops up to 1000 edges. Geometry is untouched; only connectivity is edited.
nothing — the model keeps its own triangles
3

Fan-patch the remaining boundary loops

Triangle fan from a centroid vertex across each small boundary loop PyMeshLab declined, which it does whenever a hole touches a non-manifold vertex.
nothing — only adds triangles across holes
4

MeshLib SDF rebuild

Ray-parity orientation repair (findDisorientedFaces), then a signed distance field with hole-tolerant winding-number signs, extracted by dual marching cubes. Falls through to the v1 remesh if MeshLib is unavailable.
surface is resampled — finest detail is lost
5

Binary voxel remesh

Voxelize to a dense occupancy grid, flood-fill the exterior, extract the isosurface with marching cubes. Cannot produce an open surface, so it always succeeds.
coarsest — rebuilt from a grid, UVs gone
original geometry preserved surface resampled geometry rebuilt from scratch

03

The generative core

A flow model learns a velocity field

Not “denoise step by step” — learn the straight-line velocity that carries pure noise onto the data distribution, then integrate it. Twelve Euler steps is enough.

The network predicts velocity, not noise. Training is a plain regression onto that target — which is exactly what makes fine-tuning it cheap.

04

Explored & retired · inference-time preference steering

Steer the trajectory instead of training it

The mirror of the previous slide: same flow, but forked mid-flight. Two candidates are decoded, a physics-aware judge ranks them, and the preference nudges the trajectory — with no weight updates and no preference dataset.

Why it is attractive

No training, no preference dataset, no weight updates. The judge is physics, not a learned reward — overhang angle, wall thickness and topology are measured directly on the decoded mesh.

Why it is retired

One run takes over 40 minutes on the Mac, because every candidate must be decoded to a mesh before it can be judged. The theory holds and is worth revisiting — but it needs hardware this lab does not have.
