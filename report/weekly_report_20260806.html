TRELLIS.2 Printability Fine-Tuning — Weekly Report

Branch flow-dpo-training · 2026-07-31 to 2026-08-06 · 17 commits · Apple M4, 32 GB unified memory

Abstract

Many post-processing libraries and methods were tried to improve quality and printability, adding different options for post-processing. manifold3d was found to be the best for 3D printing, and the original trimesh pipeline was kept as well. Further research was done on the best way to decrease non-manifold edges of the models, and the TriFlow paper on generating artist-like 3D models was read.

Integrating DreamDPO concepts was also tried, with no success. The DreamDPO work for diffusion models was read, but the preference discriminator was simulated as a score rather than a VLM, and the results showed no gains — it always chose the reference path for the flow model. That, together with a lack of expertise in this area of the theory, led to the decision to work instead on fine-tuning the SLat flow model using LoRA methods.

This was tried on a small sample size of 300 curated 3D models from Thingi10K. Initial training showed gains, with significantly less non-manifold edges for raw results, without loss in detail. More latent data is being collected for training.

1. Executive summary

We retired the inference-time DPO steering approach, established that supervised fine-tuning is the better instrument for this problem, built the full data and training pipeline, and completed a 1,170-step LoRA fine-tune of TRELLIS.2's shape-SLat flow model on 300 clean Thingi10K models.

The fine-tune works on the axes it can reach. Measured on exported meshes, held-out set, paired sampling noise:

Step 1050 beats the base model on thin-wall penalty for 6 of 6 held-out objects. Image fidelity did not degrade (0.897 → 0.908–0.913), and surface detail slightly increased, so this is not printability bought by smoothing the model into generic blobs.

Three caveats stated up front. (1) Overhang never moved on any checkpoint. (2) Watertightness is unchanged and cannot be changed by this training — it is a decoder property. (3) The run diverged at ~step 1100 due to a missing LR schedule; the last 70 steps are worthless and the final model is not the best model.

2. What was discovered

2.1 The retired approach failed for a diagnosable reason

Rather than using a VLM, the preference discriminator was simulated as a score. It produced no gains, and always chose the reference path for the flow model. The measurements below say the same thing from the other side.

Inference-time DPO steering produced a gradient contribution of −0.0027 ± 0.0078 over 17 scored forks — indistinguishable from zero. Root cause: the only differentiable scalar available (slat_detail_proxy) agreed with the judge's within-fork argmax 5.9% of the time against a 33% chance line, with mean Spearman −0.265. The optimiser worked; its objective was near-orthogonal to the goal.

2.2 The "differentiability wall" did not exist

The premise that mesh extraction blocks gradients is false. TRELLIS.2's decoder is dual-grid and regresses vertex positions smoothly (vertices = (1+2·margin)·sigmoid(...) − margin); only connectivity is boolean. Six weeks of design rested on an unverified assertion that took one inspection of two source files to refute.

2.3 The VAE bounds what any flow-model training can achieve

Encoding a perfectly watertight mesh and decoding it straight back:

PropertyResult
Shape fidelityPreserved — Chamfer 0.0052, p95 0.0101, bounding box exact
Watertightness0 of 2 preserved
Defect rate (full mesh)0.086% open edges, 0.095% non-manifold
Components953, but largest is 99.85% of faces — and still not watertight alone

Consequence: geometry-carried printability (thickness, overhang, bulk shape) is reachable by training the flow model; watertightness is not, and belongs to post-processing. This single 20-minute measurement redirected the entire project.

2.4 The judge inflates topology defects ~50x

geometric_judge decimates before scoring. Measured on the same mesh: 0.086% open-edge rate on the full mesh versus 4.25% after the judge's preprocessing. Every historical L_Topo figure is therefore dominated by decimation artifacts rather than real decoder defects. Any topology claim must be measured on the undecimated mesh.

2.5 Measurement noise nearly made the whole run uninterpretable

Two evaluations of the same base model gave L_OH 0.2889 and 0.3871 — a 34% swing from sampling noise alone, larger than any plausible training effect. Fixed by seeding sampling noise per held-out object and reusing it at every evaluation (common random numbers). Consecutive evals are now bit-identical, so any movement is attributable to the model.

2.7 Post-processing was addressed before generation was

Many post-processing libraries and methods were tried to improve quality and printability, with different options added to the pipeline. manifold3d was found to be the best for 3D printing and was added, with the original trimesh pipeline kept alongside it. Separate research was done on the best way to decrease non-manifold edges, and the TriFlow paper on generating artist-like 3D models was read.

This matters for reading the rest of this report: the fine-tuning results quoted here are on raw decoder output, with no post-processing applied. The reduction in non-manifold edges is therefore a gain made before manifold3d or trimesh repair runs, not instead of them. Both remain in the pipeline, and the round-trip result in §2.3 is why — watertightness is a decoder property that fine-tuning cannot reach.

Dataset

Thingi10K via its HuggingFace mirror. The corpus is deliberately a record of real-world 3D-printing messiness — its own figures are 50% non-solid, 45% self-intersecting, 26% multi-component, 22% non-manifold. Filtering to Closed + edge/vertex manifold + single component + PWN + no degenerate faces + zero self-intersections leaves 3,935 of 10,000. We used 300 (270 train / 30 held-out); median latent 2,233 voxels; 666 MB.

4. The divergence

StepsMean lossMax lossGrad norm
1–1100~0.160.800.03–0.06
1101–11703.4857.29332.25

Held-out loss went 0.0859 → 10.29; image similarity 0.908 → 0.202. The model was destroyed. Two causes, both mine: no learning-rate decay (constant 1e-4 for 8.7 epochs), and gradient clipping that bounded each update but could not prevent AdamW's moments accumulating through a sustained blow-up. Checkpoints 750/900/1050 were preserved before stopping, because a clean stop writes a final checkpoint and prune_checkpoints(keep=3) would have deleted step 750.

5. How to log improvements numerically

Recommended standard for every future comparison. The first three are non-negotiable given what we measured this week.

5.1 Always paired, never pooled

Fix the sampling noise per held-out object and reuse it across every model being compared. Report per-object deltas and a win count, not two means. Justification: 96.8% of judge-score variance is between-object, and a pooled metric previously scored raw vertex count at r=0.959 while a perfect within-fork ranker scored 0.180.

5.2 Always against a baseline

Every run begins with a step-0 evaluation of the base model with adapters disabled. A printability number without the base value on the same axes cannot indicate whether training helped.

5.3 Always report fidelity beside printability

Minimum pair: a printability metric and image_similarity (silhouette IoU against the conditioning render). A printability gain with fidelity falling is a regression. Precedent: a 2-D experiment tripled its reward while collapsing all 3,000 samples onto one geometry.

5.5 The acceptance bar

Improvement over the base model is not the bar. The bar is:

capture = (tuned_1_sample - base_1_sample) / (base_best_of_N - base_1_sample)

A single sample from the tuned model must recover a meaningful fraction of what N-fold rejection sampling buys from the base model at matched compute. This test has not yet been run.

6. Next steps

  1. Finish the topology consistency test (running: 10 objects × 3 seeds × 4 models, paired, undecimated). Answers whether step 1050's cleaner topology is consistent or a lucky draw.
  2. Run the best-of-N comparison (§5.5). Until this exists we do not know whether the fine-tune beats cheap rejection sampling — the only comparison that justifies the work.
  3. Fix the training schedule and re-run. Cosine LR decay; a skip-step rule that drops a batch whose gradient norm exceeds a multiple of the running median rather than clipping it; keep every checkpoint. The metrics were still improving when the run broke, so the ceiling was never observed.
  4. Repair the judge before it is trusted further: make the thickness raycast deterministic (test-retest reliability was measured at 0.208, capping any correlation at |r| ≤ 0.456), and add a support raycast to the overhang term (currently rank-correlates −0.432 with true unsupported area).
  5. Scale the dataset. 270 training objects is the binding constraint, not step count; 3,935 clean models are available and prep runs at ~40 s/model.
  6. Add a slicer-based support-volume metric as the held-out physical measure that was never part of the training reward.
